# UK VAT for Amazon Sellers

Snapshot as of 2026. Verify current figures with HMRC guidance or a UK VAT advisor before acting — this threshold has changed before (it moved from £85,000 to £90,000 in April 2024) and can change again.

## The threshold only applies to UK-established businesses

This is the single most important branch point, and it's easy to get backwards:

- **UK-established sellers** (business legally established in the UK): must register for VAT once **taxable turnover exceeds £90,000** in any rolling 12-month period (not a calendar year — check the trailing 12 months at any point in time). Deregistration threshold is £88,000 if turnover falls back below that.
- **Non-established taxable persons (NETPs)** — i.e., sellers based outside the UK (US, EU, elsewhere) selling into the UK — **do not get the £90,000 threshold at all**. An NETP must register for UK VAT from their **first taxable sale**, regardless of amount. This catches a lot of non-UK Amazon sellers off guard: "I'm well under £90k" is not a valid reason to delay registration if the business itself isn't UK-established.

Always establish where the business is legally established before answering a UK VAT question — this one fact flips the entire analysis.

## Online marketplace (deemed supplier) rules

Since 1 January 2021, the UK treats the online marketplace (Amazon) as the deemed supplier for VAT purposes on certain transactions, meaning Amazon — not the seller — charges and remits the VAT to HMRC. This generally applies when:
- The seller is based outside the UK, and
- The goods are located in the UK at the point of sale (e.g., in Amazon UK FBA), and
- The sale is to a UK consumer (B2C)

What this means practically:
- On qualifying sales, Amazon handles the output VAT — the seller doesn't charge VAT on that sale themselves.
- The seller can still need to be VAT registered anyway, for reasons the deemed-supplier rule doesn't cover: reclaiming **import VAT** paid when goods entered the UK, VAT on **B2B sales**, sales where goods were located outside the UK at the point of sale (so the deemed-supplier rule doesn't apply), or simply because they're an NETP and registration is required from the first sale regardless.
- Don't tell a user "Amazon handles UK VAT for you" as a blanket statement — check which category their sales fall into.

## VAT rates (for context, not exhaustive)

- **Standard rate: 20%** — most goods and services.
- **Reduced rate: 5%** — a specific list of goods (e.g., domestic energy, children's car seats).
- **Zero rate: 0%** — a specific list including most food, books, children's clothing.
Most Amazon sellers of general goods will be dealing with the standard 20% rate; flag reduced/zero-rated categories only if the user's product plausibly falls into one, and note that the specific category list should be checked rather than assumed.

## Filing basics

- VAT returns are normally filed **quarterly**.
- **Making Tax Digital (MTD)** requires VAT-registered businesses to keep digital records and file via MTD-compatible software — relevant for how the seller's bookkeeping should be structured, not just for filing itself.
- Payment is typically due at the same time as the return, roughly one calendar month plus 7 days after the end of the VAT period — treat this as a rule to confirm against the seller's actual VAT period, not a fixed date.
