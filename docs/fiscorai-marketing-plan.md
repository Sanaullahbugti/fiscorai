# FiscorAI — Marketing Plan

*Internal GTM working document, structured as a campaign brief for the "Monitor tier" wedge launch (US+UK+EU nexus monitoring, ahead of full filing capability) plus the follow-on "Guardian tier" filing launch.*

## 1. Campaign overview

- **Campaign name**: "Know Before You Owe" (Monitor-tier wedge) → "FiscorAI Files It" (Guardian-tier filing launch, ~month 9–12)
- **Summary**: Get multi-jurisdiction Amazon sellers to discover their hidden nexus exposure for free, then convert them to paid filing once that capability ships — using the free monitoring product as the trust-building on-ramp the business plan identifies as necessary given the category's inherent trust barrier.
- **Primary objective**: 1,000 free Monitor-tier signups from the target segment (Pan-EU FBA sellers + US multi-state sellers) within 4 months of Monitor-tier launch.
- **Secondary objective**: Convert 15% of engaged Monitor-tier users to a paid tier within 90 days of Guardian-tier (filing) launch.

## 2. Target audience

**Primary segment**: Amazon FBA sellers running Pan-European FBA or Multi-Country Inventory, doing roughly $500k–$5M/year, who have inventory sitting in 2+ EU countries and either don't know it or know it vaguely and are anxious about it. They discover solutions through Amazon seller Facebook groups, YouTube seller-education channels, and word of mouth in seller communities — not through traditional B2B SaaS channels like LinkedIn ads.

**Secondary segment**: US-based Amazon sellers who've crossed $500k+ in a concentrated state (California is the sharpest edge case per the build/business plans) or spread across enough states that "is Amazon handling this for me" has become a real question they can't confidently answer.

**Audience profile**: *An Amazon FBA seller doing high six to low seven figures a year who just realized (often via a scary email from a state tax authority, a warning in Seller Central, or a post in a Facebook group) that "Amazon collects my sales tax" doesn't mean their tax obligations are zero. They're currently either ignoring the problem, overpaying an accountant who doesn't understand Amazon-specific mechanics like Pan-EU inventory placement, or juggling 2–3 point-solution tools that don't talk to each other.*

**Buying stage**: Largely *problem-aware but solution-unaware* — most of this audience knows something is off but doesn't know a single-product answer exists yet, since (per the business plan's competitive research) one genuinely doesn't today. This shapes messaging toward education first, product second.

## 3. Key messages

**Core message**: *Amazon tells you what you sold. FiscorAI tells you what you owe — and files it, across the US, UK, and EU, from one place.*

**Supporting messages**:
1. *"Amazon collecting your sales tax doesn't mean you're covered."* — addresses the single most common and costly misconception validated during product research; pairs naturally with educational content.
2. *"Your FBA inventory can create a tax bill you never chose."* — Pan-EU and multi-state inventory placement as an out-of-your-control nexus trigger is the sharpest, most surprising pain point and the best top-of-funnel hook.
3. *"Stop paying for three tools that still don't file anything."* — direct comparison against the fragmented A2X/Numeral/hellotax-style stack sellers currently assemble.
4. *"Free to see what you owe. Pay only once we're ready to file it for you."* — de-risks the trust barrier by leading with the free Monitor tier, consistent with the business plan's go-to-market wedge strategy.

**Proof points**: reference thresholds and mechanics precisely (the £90,000 UK non-established-seller exception, the €10,000 EU OSS threshold that doesn't apply to non-EU sellers, California's 25%-of-sales franchise tax trigger) — specificity is itself the credibility signal in this audience, since generic "we handle your taxes" copy is what every competitor already says.

## 9. Risks and mitigations

- **Risk**: Community-first channels (Facebook groups, Reddit) penalize anything that reads as promotional, and this audience is unusually alert to vendors pretending to be neutral commenters. **Mitigation**: founder-led, transparently-identified participation with genuinely useful answers even when they don't mention FiscorAI; treat promotion as a rare, earned exception, not the default.
- **Risk**: Comparison content against named competitors (Link My Books, TaxJar, hellotax) risks looking like a strawman if it misrepresents what they actually do. **Mitigation**: base every comparison claim on the same competitive research underlying the business plan, and be explicit about what competitors do well (e.g., Link My Books' bookkeeping sync is genuinely good at what it does) rather than only listing gaps.
- **Risk**: Free Monitor-tier signups may not convert once Guardian-tier filing launches, if the monitoring experience alone satisfies the anxiety that drove signup. **Mitigation**: design the Monitor-tier product experience (build plan) to make the *unresolved* nature of a flagged nexus issue visible and mildly uncomfortable — not manipulative, just honest about "this is still unresolved" — so the filing tier's value is obvious when it ships.
