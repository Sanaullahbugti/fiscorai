# Do You Need to Register Anywhere? (Short answer: Yes — mainly California, and it's not about sales tax)

Amazon collecting and remitting sales tax solves **one** problem — sales/use tax collection — but it does not solve **registration** or **income/franchise tax** obligations. Those are separate issues, and based on your numbers you almost certainly have exposure in California that has nothing to do with sales tax at all.

## 1. Sales tax collection — Amazon has this covered (mostly)

Every state that imposes sales tax now has a **marketplace facilitator law**. Amazon is registered as a marketplace facilitator in all of them and is legally required to calculate, collect, and remit sales tax on marketplace transactions — including FBA sales — on your behalf. Because 100% of your revenue runs through Amazon (not your own website, not wholesale, not other channels), you generally:

- Do **not** need a separate seller's permit/sales tax registration in states where Amazon is your only sales channel, including California and the dozen "spread thin" states.
- Are not responsible for calculating or remitting the sales tax Amazon already collected.

The one caveat: if you ever start selling through your own Shopify site, wholesale, trade shows, etc., those non-marketplace sales are your responsibility to tax and register for separately in any state where you have nexus.

## 2. The part Amazon is NOT handling: income/franchise tax and business registration

This is the piece most FBA sellers miss. Marketplace facilitator laws cover *sales tax* only. They say nothing about:

- State **income tax** or **franchise/LLC tax** nexus
- **"Doing business" registration** with a state's Secretary of State
- **Business licenses**, gross receipts taxes (like Ohio's CAT), or annual report filings

Nexus for these is triggered independently, either by economic activity (dollar/percentage thresholds) or by physical presence — and FBA inventory sitting in Amazon warehouses counts as physical presence in whatever state that warehouse is in.

## 3. California — this is your real exposure

At $520,000 of your $650,000 in revenue, California is the one that matters:

- California's "doing business" / economic nexus standard (R&TC §23101) is triggered if in-state sales exceed the **lesser of** ~$735,000 (2024 inflation-adjusted figure) **or 25% of total sales**. Your CA sales are 80% of your total revenue ($520k / $650k) — far above the 25% test — so you clearly trip the lower threshold. You are "doing business" in California for franchise tax purposes regardless of whether Amazon stores any inventory there.
- Practical consequence: as a single-member LLC formed in Ohio, you're required to **register as a foreign LLC with the California Secretary of State**, file a **California Form 568** (LLC return) each year, and pay:
  - The **$800 annual minimum franchise tax**, plus
  - The **CA LLC fee**, which at $500,000–$999,999 of California-sourced income is currently **$2,500/year**.
- So the realistic bill is roughly **$3,300/year to California**, plus the administrative burden of foreign qualification, a registered agent in CA, and annual filings — separate and apart from anything Amazon is doing with sales tax.
- On the sales tax side specifically, California (CDTFA) generally does **not** require marketplace sellers to hold a separate seller's permit if their only CA sales go through a registered marketplace facilitator and their only CA presence is FBA inventory — so you likely don't need a CA sales tax permit. But the franchise tax/LLC registration piece above is a different requirement and does apply.

## 4. The other dozen states — lower risk, but check two things

For the states where revenue is "spread thin":

- **Sales tax**: Amazon is handling collection/remittance as marketplace facilitator, same as California. No action needed there in most cases.
- **Income/franchise tax nexus**: Each state has its own economic nexus threshold for income tax purposes (many mirror the old $100,000-sales / 200-transaction Wayfair-era sales tax thresholds, but not all — some are lower, some higher, and a few states have no income tax at all, e.g., TX, FL, WA, NV, WY, SD, TN, AK, NH). If any single state's revenue crosses its own income tax nexus threshold, you could owe a return there even though your total is small. This needs a state-by-state check.
- **FBA physical presence**: This is the sneaky one. Amazon doesn't necessarily store your inventory only in states where you have sales — its fulfillment network can place your inventory in warehouses in states where you have **zero customers**. Physical inventory in a state is a classic nexus trigger for income tax purposes in most states (independent of dollar thresholds). Pull your **Inventory Event Detail report** from Seller Central to see every state your stock has actually sat in over the past 12 months — that list is often longer than your dozen "sales" states.

## 5. Ohio (home state)

You're presumably already registered there as the LLC's formation/home state. One thing worth checking: Ohio's Commercial Activity Tax (CAT) exclusion threshold was raised significantly starting 2024 (to $3 million of taxable gross receipts, and $6 million starting 2025), so most small sellers no longer owe CAT — but confirm your registration status is current given the law change.

## Bottom line

| Obligation | Amazon's marketplace-facilitator collection cover it? |
|---|---|
| Sales tax collection/remittance | Yes, in essentially all states, including CA |
| Sales tax seller's permit registration | Generally not needed if Amazon is your only channel |
| California LLC foreign qualification + Form 568 + franchise tax/LLC fee | **No — you likely owe this now** |
| Income/franchise tax in other high-volume or inventory-storage states | **No — needs a state-by-state nexus check** |
| Ohio CAT / home-state filings | Not related to Amazon at all — separate obligation |

**Recommended next steps:**
1. Register your LLC as a foreign entity in California, file Form 568, and pay the $800 tax + $2,500 LLC fee (or get a CPA to confirm the exact fee bracket and any prior-year exposure — this can go back further than you'd like if you've been over the threshold before).
2. Pull Amazon's Inventory Event Detail report to identify every state where your inventory has physically sat.
3. Have a CPA who specializes in multistate e-commerce/FBA sellers run a proper nexus study covering income tax (not just sales tax) across the states from steps 1–2, since exposure and penalties can quietly accumulate year over year if unaddressed.
4. Don't extrapolate California's rules to other states — thresholds and "doing business" definitions vary widely, and a few states (like TX, WA, NV) don't have a personal/individual-style income tax pass-through issue the way CA does, which changes the calculus per state.

*This is general information, not personalized tax or legal advice. Given the dollar amounts involved (particularly the California exposure), it's worth a paid consultation with a CPA or tax attorney experienced in multistate e-commerce before filing anything or before your next franchise tax deadline.*
