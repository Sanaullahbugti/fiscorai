Short answer: yes, you need to register for VAT in Germany and Poland now, and no, the €10,000 threshold does not help you — it doesn't even apply to a seller in your position.

## Why

**The €10,000 threshold is an EU-established-seller benefit only.** It lets sellers based *in* the EU sell cross-border to consumers in other member states while charging their *home* country's VAT rate, up to €10,000/year in combined distance sales. As a Delaware LLC with no UK or EU presence, you're a non-EU-established seller — you don't get this threshold at all. There's no "under the threshold, use home rate" option for you, because you have no EU home rate to default to. VAT exposure for a non-EU seller starts from the first sale/first unit of relevant activity, not after crossing €10,000.

**Pan-EU FBA is a separate and bigger trigger than the threshold anyway.** The moment Amazon physically stores your inventory in a country under Pan-European FBA or Multi-Country Inventory, that alone creates a VAT registration obligation in that country — independent of your sales volume and independent of the €10,000 OSS threshold entirely. The threshold governs distance *sales*; it says nothing about where you *store* goods. A single unit sitting in a Polish fulfillment center is enough to trigger a Polish VAT registration duty.

## Jurisdiction status

| Jurisdiction | Status | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Germany | Register now | Amazon is storing your inventory there under Pan-EU — registration is triggered by storage alone, not by sales volume or any threshold. |
| Poland | Register now | Same logic — inventory physically present there under Pan-EU. |
| €10,000 OSS threshold | Does not apply to you | Reserved for EU-established sellers. As a non-EU (US) seller, you get no threshold cushion — VAT obligations in the relevant country attach from the start. |
| Other Pan-EU countries (e.g. France, Italy, Spain, Czech Republic) | Monitor | Amazon can redistribute Pan-EU stock into additional fulfillment centers over time without much visibility to you. Pull your Seller Central inventory reports periodically — each new country with your stock in it is another mandatory registration, not an optional one. |
| OSS registration | Separate consideration | If you sell cross-border from your German/Polish stock to consumers in *other* EU countries, OSS lets you report those distance sales in one quarterly return instead of registering locally in every destination country. But OSS does **not** replace the Germany/Poland registrations — those exist because of physical inventory storage, and OSS only covers distance-sale reporting, not storage-triggered obligations. |

## Bottom line

You likely need VAT registrations in Germany and Poland now, and a real possibility of more registrations as Amazon shifts Pan-EU inventory around — the €10,000 threshold is irrelevant to your situation on two independent grounds (you're not EU-established, and the trigger here is inventory storage, not distance-sales volume). I'd treat "which countries currently hold my stock" as something to check in Seller Central regularly rather than a one-time question, since Pan-EU inventory placement is largely automated and out of your direct control.

One nuance worth flagging before you register anywhere: check whether any of your EU sales already have Amazon collecting/remitting VAT as deemed supplier (this applies in certain marketplace-facilitated scenarios, similar to the UK's deemed-supplier rule) — that can affect what you're reporting on your own returns even where you're still required to hold the registration itself. Don't assume "Amazon already handles it" without verifying per transaction type.
