# Pan-European FBA and VAT: Do You Need to Register?

**Short answer: Yes, you almost certainly need to register for VAT in Germany and Poland right now (and possibly more countries), and the €10,000 threshold does not help you at all — it doesn't apply to your situation.**

Below is the reasoning, laid out so you can see exactly why, plus what to do next. This is general information, not tax/legal advice — given the number of moving parts (and penalties for getting it wrong), you should confirm the specifics with an EU VAT specialist before or immediately after you proceed.

---

## 1. Why storing stock creates an immediate VAT obligation

VAT registration obligations in the EU are triggered by two different things, and it's easy to conflate them:

- **Distance selling to consumers** (B2C sales shipped cross-border to a customer) — this is what the €10,000 threshold and the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme are about.
- **Holding/storing inventory in a country** — this is a completely separate trigger, and it has **no threshold at all**. The moment you have goods physically sitting in a warehouse in an EU country, that country considers you to have a taxable presence there, and you're required to register for VAT — even if you sell zero units from that warehouse.

Amazon's Pan-European FBA program works by redistributing your inventory across its EU fulfillment network (commonly Germany, Poland, Czech Republic, France, Italy, and Spain, depending on which marketplaces/program options you've enabled) so it can ship faster and cheaper. That means Amazon itself is creating VAT registration obligations for you in every country it decides to place your stock — often without much visibility or control on your end.

**Bottom line:** Because you now have stock in Germany and Poland, you need to register for German VAT and Polish VAT. This isn't optional, and it isn't something the €10,000 threshold can excuse you from.

---

## 2. Why the €10,000 threshold doesn't help you

The EU-wide €10,000 distance-selling threshold (introduced July 1, 2021, as part of the EU's e-commerce VAT reform) works like this: if you are a business **established within the EU**, and your total cross-border B2C sales to consumers in *other* EU countries stay under €10,000/year, you're allowed to charge VAT at your home country's rate instead of registering in each destination country.

Two reasons this doesn't apply to you:

1. **It's only for EU-established sellers.** You're a Delaware LLC with no EU establishment. Non-EU sellers get no threshold at all — the obligation to register applies from the first sale/first unit of stock, with no minimum.
2. **It only concerns the distance-selling/OSS question (which country's VAT rate to charge on a cross-border sale), not the stock-storage question.** Even an EU-established seller under the €10,000 threshold would *still* need local VAT registration in any country where they physically store inventory — the threshold never overrides that.

So the €10,000 figure is simply irrelevant to your situation on both counts.

---

## 3. What you actually need to do

**A. Register for local VAT in every country where Amazon stores your stock**
- Germany and Poland, at minimum, based on what you've described.
- Check your Seller Central inventory reports (Pan-European FBA Inventory / "Inventory Event Detail") regularly — Amazon can shift stock into additional Pan-EU countries (Czech Republic, France, Italy, Spain, and others) over time as it optimizes logistics, and each new country triggers a new registration requirement.
- **Poland requires non-EU businesses to appoint a fiscal representative** (a local entity that is jointly liable for your VAT compliance) — this is a real cost and administrative step, not just paperwork. Germany generally does not require a fiscal representative for US businesses, but you'll still need a local tax agent/accountant to handle filings.

**B. Register for and use the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme for your cross-border B2C sales**
- Once you're VAT-registered in an EU country due to stock (even without being "established" there), EU rules allow you to use OSS through that member state as your "Member State of Identification."
- OSS lets you report VAT on B2C sales that ship *across* EU borders (e.g., a sale fulfilled from your German warehouse to a customer in France) in a single quarterly return, instead of registering in every customer's country.
- **Important: OSS does not replace your local registrations.** You still need separate German and Polish VAT registrations to (1) account for domestic sales within those countries, and (2) report the "deemed intra-community transfers" described below.

**C. Report the stock movements themselves**
- When Amazon moves your own inventory from, say, Germany to Poland under Pan-EU, that movement is treated as a deemed intra-community supply (in the country of dispatch) and a deemed intra-community acquisition (in the country of arrival) — even though no sale has occurred yet. This needs to be reported (EC Sales Lists / recapitulative statements) in addition to normal VAT returns. Amazon's VAT transaction reports are the standard source data for this.

**D. Customs/import side**
- You'll need an EORI number if you don't already have one, since you're importing goods from the US into the EU.

**E. Timing matters**
- Ideally, VAT registration should be in place *before* stock physically lands in a new country. Since you've "just turned on" Pan-EU, treat this as urgent — backdated/late registration can trigger interest and penalties in some countries, and Amazon can also suspend your ability to use Pan-EU FBA in a marketplace if you can't demonstrate valid VAT numbers when asked.

---

## 4. Practical next steps

1. Pull your current Pan-EU inventory placement report in Seller Central to get the definitive, current list of countries holding your stock (don't rely solely on "Germany and Poland" if the program has since spread further).
2. Register for VAT in Germany and Poland immediately (Poland via a fiscal representative).
3. Once registered, enroll in the Union OSS scheme to simplify reporting of your cross-border distance sales.
4. Get an EORI number if you don't have one.
5. Set up a process (or use a provider) to monitor Amazon's inventory reports monthly, since Pan-EU can add new storage countries without much notice — each one is a new registration trigger.
6. Consider Amazon's "VAT Services on Amazon" program (discounted/free registration and filing support for the first year in participating countries) or a third-party EU VAT compliance firm (e.g., Avalara, hellotax, Taxdoo, SimplyVAT) — most sellers doing Pan-EU FBA outsource this because the number of jurisdictions and filing calendars gets unwieldy fast.

## Summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Do I need to register for VAT? | Yes — in Germany and Poland now, and in any other country Amazon stores your stock. |
| Does the €10,000 threshold help? | No — it only applies to EU-established sellers' cross-border sales, and never overrides the stock-storage registration trigger. |
| Does OSS replace local registration? | No — OSS simplifies reporting of cross-border B2C sales but you still need local VAT numbers in every country holding your stock. |
| Anything else? | EORI number, possible fiscal representative in Poland, and reporting of intra-EU stock transfers (not just sales). |

Given the stakes (potential penalties, Amazon account/listing risk, and retroactive VAT liability), I'd strongly recommend engaging an EU VAT specialist or a service like Amazon's VAT Services program this week rather than treating this as something to get to eventually.
